01 / PROBLEM STATEMENT

Why this work exists

A visually good curve fit does not establish forecast value. The research asks whether parsimonious and machine-learning term-structure models improve on persistence or mean reversion without seeing future information.

Data and source material

  • Moroccan sovereign yield curves from Bank Al-Maghrib
  • Euro-area AAA curves from the ECB
  • U.S. Treasury yields for cross-market experiments
  • Macro series with explicit unit and publication-date controls

02 / SOLUTION OVERVIEW

From input to usable output

A documented pipeline compares parsimonious factor methods with nonlinear alternatives and mean-reversion baselines.

  1. 01

    Normalize units and align source calendars

  2. 02

    Extract Nelson–Siegel or PCA factors at each forecast origin

  3. 03

    Fit Dynamic Nelson–Siegel, regularized time-series, mean-reversion, and nonlinear alternatives

  4. 04

    Generate expanding-window forecasts

  5. 05

    Compare maturity and horizon errors with simple benchmarks

04 / METHODOLOGY & IMPLEMENTATION

Decisions that shape the system

  • Nelson–Siegel represents each curve through level, slope, and curvature factors.
  • Forecast pipelines use origin-local factor extraction, scaling, and tuning to prevent future leakage.
  • Persistence and simple mean reversion remain first-class benchmarks.
  • Overlapping forecast windows are handled explicitly in Diebold–Mariano comparisons.

Implemented capabilities

  • Nelson–Siegel factors
  • Dynamic forecasts
  • Maturity-specific evaluation
  • Cross-market comparison
  • Long-horizon validation

05 / RESULTS & EVIDENCE

What the source supports

  • The expanded BAM evaluation records one-year RMSE of approximately 64.6 bp for publication-time DNS, 64.8 bp for simple NS AR(1), and 67.1 bp for persistence.
  • Persistence is best at the five-year horizon.
  • The repository describes longer forecasts as equilibrium scenarios rather than precision pricing claims.

Results preserve the repository’s units, evaluation framing, and stated limitations. Missing evidence is not converted into a claim.

06 / CHALLENGES & LIMITATIONS

Where the evidence stops

  • Overlapping long-horizon forecasts limit statistical power.
  • Results depend on curve publication history, frequency, and unit handling.
  • A small improvement over persistence does not imply a tradable strategy or precise bond-price forecast.
MISSING EVIDENCE

No live trading result or production portfolio attribution; internship-confidential material is excluded.

Engineering signal
